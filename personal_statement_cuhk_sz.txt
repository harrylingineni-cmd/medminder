My math teacher once told me: "Perfection belongs only to God. Someone will always be closer to 100 than you; that gap is what fuels you to keep building instead of settling." I was the first student from SMP Katolik Stella Maris Tomohon to reach KSN nationals in junior high. I lost — not because I was outmatched, but because my own overconfidence blinded me. I nearly quit competitive mathematics entirely. My teacher's reframing — a ranking is information, not a verdict — pulled me back. OSN, WMI, SASMO, and IOB followed.

That lesson shapes how I approach engineering: the goal is not a flawless artifact, but a system that works under real constraints. My academic record reflects this discipline. Across six semesters I hold a 95.63 overall average and a 97.87 STEM-only average — Mathematics 98–99, Physics 98–100, Informatics 95–100, Chemistry 95–98, Biology 96–99, Economics 98–100, every semester without gaps. I consistently rank top 3–4 in my school.

I have spent years translating that discipline into shipped code and soldered hardware. Teaching myself Lua, I built UI widgets for the open-source RTS engine Beyond All Reason. For Project Zomboid, I designed and published the Global Occult Coalition Radio system on Steam Workshop — 41 ratings, live users, iterative updates driven by community feedback. In robotics, I hand-soldered a custom PCB for the SURO Cup using Arduino components, no kit, no reference design. Flux everywhere, terminals tight enough a jury member asked if it would short. I traced every line myself, held it together with tape, and it worked. MediGuard, a Flutter/Dart health-tech application built with a collaborator, is live on Google Play Store. On Kaggle (username: Nekkixn), the Titanic project is in progress — ongoing, not finished, not pushed. A planned water-quality classification system, tied to maintaining my family's koi pond and orchard, remains on the roadmap.

These experiences taught me that engineering lives in the gap between specification and substrate — noise, tolerance, power budgets, legacy APIs, human error. CUHK-Shenzhen's Artificial Intelligence Stream within the BSc Computer Science and Engineering program, housed in the School of Data Science, aligns precisely with where I want to deepen that practice. The Columbia University 3+2 Initiative — with fourteen participating master's programs — offers a concrete pathway to advanced research without abandoning the ecosystem I value most: Shenzhen and the Greater Bay Area. DJI, Tencent, and a hackathon culture that rewards shipping over posturing make this location a laboratory, not a label.

I do not chase perfection. I chase the next build that survives contact with reality. CUHK-Shenzhen is where I want to make that happen.
